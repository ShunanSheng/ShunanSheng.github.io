About me

I am a Ph.D. student in Statistics at Columbia University, advised by Professors Marcel Nutz and Alberto González-Sanz. My research interests lie in optimal transport, robust finance, variational inference, and sampling.

I have been supported by the Bloomberg Quantitative Finance Ph.D. Fellowship since September 2024. I also spent the summers of 2025 and 2026 as a quantitative research intern at Bloomberg.

Before Columbia, I received my B.S. in Mathematical Sciences from Nanyang Technological University in Singapore, where I was advised by Professor Ariel Neufeld.

I am on the 2026–27 academic job market.

Research themes

My work connects the mathematical foundations of probability and optimization with questions in statistics and quantitative finance.

  • Optimal transport and statistics. Linearization, statistical limits, and robustness of transport potentials and transport-based quantiles. [1] [2] [7]
  • Robust finance. Martingale optimal transport and robust pricing with bid–ask spreads. [3]
  • Variational inference and sampling. Stability, mode collapse, and the theory and computation of structured variational approximations. [4] [5] [6]
